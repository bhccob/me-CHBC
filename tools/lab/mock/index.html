Opening mock-ups

Browser mock-ups made before any build: the coin from Bob's brief (11 Nov 2026), and two more ideas in the same spirit. Click a frame to open it live; the first view reacts to the cursor, and its O turns when you hover or drag it. Stills come from tools/lab/mock/shoot.mjs.

Round 51 · The studies, quick and named; the brain in its head

Bob on round 50: the brain must be inside the robot's head; the coins took far too long; it should be plain that the coins are the studies; the machine design is nice. Now: the first coin drops in and the machine switches on. The four study coins come in quick, one after another (about a screen of scrolling for all four), each with its name beside it as it falls; as it goes into the slot the screen shows its mark, number and full name, and it drops into its own tube in the window, the study's name written up the side of the tube, which lights. When all four are in it smiles ("Now it thinks"), the camera rises to its head, the screen clears, and inside the head is its brain, sitting in the square hole of the coin you came through, fed by four lit lines from the studies. You go in to it. Open it live (scroll all the way).

Round 50 · A coin machine, all on scroll

Bob: the coin transition is clean; the robot area and the coins going in did not feel right. His answers: the study coins should fall in by scroll like the first one, and the robot should be a machine where putting a coin in makes sense, without the glass look. The robot is now a coin machine (machine.py): a purple painted cabinet with brass trim, a screen for a head (the works, then its eyes), round speaker ears and an antenna, a shelf under the screen with one slot and an INSERT COIN plate, and a window in its body. Behind the window: brass rails, rail to rail, down to a row of four bays and the CPU socket in the middle, where the brain sits. The first coin drops in, rolls the rails and seats in the CPU socket round the brain; the screen switches off and its eyes come on. Keep scrolling: each study coin falls in from above, rolls the same rails into its own bay, and the screen reads it. Then you go in through the window, into the coin's square hole, to the brain. No pressing. Open it live (scroll all the way).
