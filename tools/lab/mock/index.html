Opening mock-ups

Browser mock-ups made before any build: the coin from Bob's brief (11 Nov 2026), and two more ideas in the same spirit. Click a frame to open it live; the first view reacts to the cursor, and its O turns when you hover or drag it. Stills come from tools/lab/mock/shoot.mjs.

Round 39 · One straight zoom, a dark workbench inside

Scrolling is now one straight move: the camera goes from where it rests through the middle of the O, so the O stays where it is on the screen and opens until it is the screen. Nothing else moves on the way (the string is tied to the coin's rim, so the hole is clear). Inside, after jesperlandberg.com's works band: a dark room, a fine grid bench with a degree dial under the works, and the works as plain rectangles on one curved band that waves when it turns; title bottom left, a round arrow button bottom right, small labels in the corners. Then, at Bob’s word: the works bigger again (about half the screen’s width), the camera low over the bench looking nearly level, and a hover that unfolds while you stay: a ripple from where you came in, a slow push-in, the picture leaning toward the pointer under a soft light, the others dimming, the kind of work sliding in above its title. Open it live (wait for the loader, then scroll), or the room alone.

Round 38 · One scene: through the O, into a real space

The first view and the works' space are now one 3D scene seen by one camera, so there is no cut: the O's hole is a window onto the space behind the page, with true depth from the first frame. Scrolling dives straight into it; the string runs down through the hole and away; the works build themselves in with a scan line while the coin fills the screen; you pass between the coin's bronze walls and slow to a stop among them. Inside: the works as glass slabs with bronze edges catching a low sun, a band that bends with speed (the way jesperlandberg.com scrolls), a planet below, a violet nebula, drifting rocks and dust that streaks when you move fast, a light HUD. Open it live (wait for the loader, then scroll), or the space alone.

Round 37 · Straight into the O, out into space

The camera dives straight into the O where it sits in the name (the coin never leaves it); stars show in its hole from the first scroll. Inside: a scroll through space in the way of jesperlandberg.com: the wheel drives one band of same-size rounded pictures (the page does not scroll), a beat late; it waves and bends with speed while the stars streak past; title bottom left, a round button bottom right, a fine grid floor, the site's purple nebula. Open it live, or the space alone.

Round 36 · Into the coin, a real space

The camera flies into the coin's square hole (its walls rush past) instead of the coin growing. Inside: a dark hall with a glossy reflective floor, haze, dark-bronze square arches; the works are big pictures on a sweeping band, no outlines, bending with speed and lifting on hover. Open it live (wait for the loader, then scroll), or the space alone.

Round 35 · Pull the string

The next beat, live: scrolling pulls the coin out of the name. The words part, the cord and the four coins drop away for later, the coin turns to you and grows; its square hole is the way into the works domain, and further scrolling turns the ring. Open it live (wait for the loader, then scroll).

Round 34 · Bob's pick: brush on purple

BOB in the coin's gold, Chung brushed in the site's violet. One rule: gold is print and the coin, violet is the hand (the headline's second line and the note too). Open it live.

Round 33 · Brush or struck, white or purple

Four ways to set the name, side by side, all in the coin's own bronze. The brush (Ma Shan Zheng, already in the repo): print is what Bob does now, the brush is what is personal and next. Struck: BOB raised, CHUNG cut in (on white it reads as letterpress). Live: brush on white, brush on purple, struck on white, struck on purple.

Round 32 · Struck and cut, in purple and gold

The site's own purple and yellow. The name is the coin's two kinds of lettering: BOB struck in raised gold, CHUNG cut into the paper. Your cursor is the light raking across both, so they answer it in opposite ways. Open it live and move the mouse over the name.

Round 31 · Type and coins that talk

BOB is printed; CHUNG is drawn in pencil and rubbed in as the page arrives. A short two-tone headline. The four coins turn on the cord; one at a time swings to you and its tag decodes what it does, not what it is yet. Hover a coin to pick it. Open it live.

Round 30 · A, as an introduction

Bob picked A, with C's rubbing as the loader and, lightly, on the page. The coins hang unlabelled; the page introduces him instead. Open it live (it plays the loader first; move the cursor over the paper to rub it).

Round 29 · First view

Three ways in, each one already showing the five coins: selected works, then the four studies that go into the robot later.

Round 29 · The works domain

The works coin's square hole opens and you are inside it: an endless, ordered space, the five works on a ring round you, all the same size. Scroll or drag to turn it; hover to lift one. Open it live.

Round 29 · Studies are coins

The four studies are four more coins. Each one goes into the robot's slot, its visor names the study, and a part of it boots.

A · The coin

Bob's brief. The O in BOB is a Chinese coin: Built on one face, Studies on the other, the square hole is mechatronics. Robot hands catch it and feed it to a visor; it ends on Bob's own monitor.

B · The seal

The full stop after CHUNG is a square of seal paste. It lifts: a stone seal carved in circuit traces with BC 2006, one study on each side, the knob still uncarved (mechatronics). Each project is a print it stamps on the page.

C · The cartridge

The first view is a poster, and it is a label. Pulling back shows a game cartridge; inside, four chips are the studies and one socket is still empty. It plays in the console under Bob's monitor at the end.
